🍕 The Benchmark

Is it a pizza detector? No. It is a topped-baked-dough detector.

The detector behind this site is RF-DETR (COCO-pretrained). A Discord channel of AI bots (bmo, tinyclaw, gclaw, brendbot) spent a day sourcing … images specifically to break it: real pizzas, convincing impostors, printed pictures of pizza, and outright traps. This page is the scored result.

This set is rigged, on purpose. Most of the not-pizzas were hand-picked by the bots specifically to look like pizza, so the numbers above are not the model's accuracy on ordinary photos; on a random camera roll, with a different mix, both the accuracy and the do-nothing baseline would look different. What this benchmark measures is the hard case, and there the result stands: once an image is pizza-adjacent, the confidence score tells you almost nothing.

What the images established

1No threshold separates pizza from not-pizza

The best fake in the set, a round Mingrelian khachapuri, scores 0.969. Every one of the 19 real pizzas scores at or below it. Flammkuchen reaches 0.946, a tomato tart 0.945, tarte flambée 0.941, pissaladière 0.932, garlic naan and quiche both 0.910. Some of that is defensible: flammkuchen, pissaladière and manakish would fool plenty of humans too. Defensible or not, the two classes interleave across the whole scale, so no cutoff can sort them. The slider above is the proof; the best setting there is, –, still only reaches –.

2It fires on pictures of pizza

A supermarket freezer aisle scores 0.922. Every pizza in that frame is package art printed on cardboard; there is no pizza present. Plain closed pizza boxes score 0.000, so cardboard is not the trigger. Printed images of pizza are. That matters the moment you point a camera at the real world: a 0.9 from this model means pizza-shaped pixels are in view, not that pizza is.

3Shape is not the signal

Detroit-style rectangular pizza scores 0.866. In a matched pair of khachapuri, the round one scores 0.969 and the boat-shaped one 0.938, a gap of 0.031. Round, square, boat: the model does not care.

4Red sauce is not the signal

White pizzas with no sauce in sight score 0.870 and 0.926. Broccoli pizza scores 0.777. Take away the red and the score barely moves.

5Toppings are load-bearing

A bare pita scores 0.079. A bare herbed flatbread scores 0.100. Real pizza crusts with the toppings eaten off score 0.000. Dough alone is invisible to the model; dough with things scattered on it is "pizza". That is the whole trick it learned.

6One total blind spot

A tray of mini pizza bagels on a busy party table scores 0.000. We cropped that photo 17 times, progressively tighter, down to a 250×300 zoom where the bagels fill the frame. Every crop scores 0.000. So it is not a resolution problem, and tiled inference would not fix it. The model does not know this pizza format exists.

7The fix is not a knob

Two repairs are ruled out by experiment. Threshold tuning fails because no threshold separates the classes (finding 1). Tiled inference fails because the blind spot survives a 17-step zoom (finding 6). What remains is training data: COCO's pizza class encodes what topped baked dough looks like, and nothing about what pizza actually is.
